Sanger joint targets and bounded deletion search — 2026-10-29

Scope
1–4 actual submitted amplicons, one shared sequencing primer and matched unedited control/sample chromatograms. Up to two cuts per product. The original one-target small-indel engines remain the default; joint analysis activates for two targets or explicit large-deletion search.

Research basis
Bloh et al., DECODR (2021), DOI:10.1089/crispr.2020.0022, describes staged net-indel class selection, subsequent refinement of edit proposals and nonnegative Lasso. The paper demonstrates two-guide excision and large/compound deletion examples. Its software section offers source-code access by inquiry; no public source repository was identified in this review. This implementation is independent and does not reproduce DECODR's Lasso coefficients, p-values or insertion-base inference.

Implementation
The actual primer determines strand and extension coordinates. Complete edited molecule models carry cumulative displacement from the first target into the second; overlapping target windows are fitted as one observation region. Close target pairs (distance <= 2*small-indel range+20) are blocked, with a non-overlapping window limit supplied. The check also reports the maximum centered window allowed by supplied products/read bounds. WT reference geometry is checked before fitting; candidate identifiability is evaluated again after selection.

Small indels cover +/-1–15 bp at each target, including joint combinations. A two-cut excision model allows junction offsets within the small-indel allowance and unresolved inserted lengths up to that allowance. Explicit large-deletion search covers up to a user-selected 500 bp. Initial deletion classes use cut-touching left/center/right junctions. Supported large-deletion classes are refined over cut-touching junction positions. Actual product coverage, preserved upstream anchoring and a browser-memory budget restrict the candidate set. Counts/exclusions are retained. Search limits are not claims of exhaustive recovery.

Sparse forward candidate selection and nonnegative sum-to-one regression use 60% training and 20% tuning positions. The remaining 20% is held out for final trace QC. WT-derived gain/phase/context calibration uses matched upstream/control signal. This is an engineering prototype, not a validated clinical estimator. Unknown inserted bases are represented as N, and insertion junction positions are excluded from the fitting observations.

Outputs
Ref-specific target edited fractions, joint event groups, total-signal group fractions, input-reference event boundaries, net size, alternative indistinguishable explanations, annotation and JSON/CSV exports. A compensating -1/+1 event is edited, despite net size zero. Only invariant reference/target/size statements are reported for equivalent models; exact unresolved boundaries are not fabricated in annotation. Failed held-out QC withholds within-reference editing estimates. Net-size spectra can combine distinct edited and WT molecules of the same net size and must not be interpreted as a read-level genotype table.

Limits
Two targets maximum; primer-derived coordinate assumptions and matched controls are required. Deletion-induced loss of a primer site/PCR dropout is unobservable. Fractions describe amplified trace contribution, not cell frequencies. Sequence similarity alone is not a separability criterion. Models outside the bounded search may cause poor fit or mimic included models; QC cannot guarantee detecting every unmodeled event. Multi-homeolog separation, exact breakpoint identification and per-target phasing are separate questions. Passing synthetic tests is not wet-lab validation.

2026-10-29 full usable-range correction
Pooled and joint mixture fitting use usable readStart/readEnd and actual chromatogram coverage, rather than the union of reporting windows. Nominal target-window geometry remains available for final interpretation. Joint proposals retain terminated molecules with zero emissions beyond their physical ends; normalized cycle totals handle unequal product lengths. A supported reference/net-size class can be refitted to one complete junction candidate when it improves the combined training+tuning objective, retaining the other classes. The final QC fold is not used for that replacement. This corrects forward-only searches that keep multiple inferior junctions after adding refined candidates; it is not a case-specific breakpoint rule. The original asymmetric-junction regression and three new backbone/deletion-size regressions are retained. These are engineering tests, not independent wet-lab validation. Full fitted groups are immutable; post-fit window summaries retain the full-fit denominator and do not promote unresolved target estimates.
