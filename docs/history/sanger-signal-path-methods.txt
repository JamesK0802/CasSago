Experimental signal-guided Sanger paths — 2026-10-29

Scope
Explicit opt-in alongside the existing staged analyzer, which remains the default. One sequencing primer, 1–4 submitted full amplicons, up to two target sites per reference. This is a reference-guided mixture optimizer, not separation of physical reads or fully reference-free chromatogram reconstruction.

Inference
Primer-axis registration and control-derived emission calibration precede affine match/insertion/deletion dynamic alignment. Training-position observed A/C/G/T intensities and current mixture residuals guide path discovery. Guide positions act as a soft prior in global/hybrid modes; local mode restricts edits to guide +/-25 bp. Insertions use observed supported bases, retaining N when unresolved. Independent sparse nonnegative fitting selects components, followed by complexity penalization and backward pruning. Hybrid additionally seeds small single-cut edit models without enumerating the full joint allele product. This is an independently implemented Viterbi-style optimizer, not a posterior pair-HMM or a reproduction of BCV/DECODR.

Bounds and uncertainty
Deletion bound up to 500 bp; insertion bound up to 60 bp. A cumulative offset band and two-million-cell search budget also apply. Paths outside configured limits are not exhaustively resolved. Equivalent sequence aliases are retained; semantic reference/target/net-size ambiguity withholds rates. 60/20/20 positional training/tuning/final-QC split. Convergence, conditioning, search-boundary, held-out R2 >=0.995, peak-cycle integrity, and local unexplained-signal checks gate reporting. Local disagreement exceeding max(0.06, matched-control RMSE+0.04) withholds editing rates. These thresholds are engineering choices, not clinical or instrument-calibrated guarantees.

Study
32 development, 288 first held-out, 144 fresh confirmation pairs: 36 scenarios, three homeologs, two guides, four conditions (one real wheat triad with two guide pairs and two constructed divergence regimes on a shared backbone). The first held-out set revealed accepted errors and informed a QC-only addendum; its results are preserved and are not final-QC-independent validation. Final confirmation uses disjoint random seeds. Signal-hybrid accepted 47/144, all 47 within prespecified share/size/target tolerances; 86 withheld and 11 blocked. This low coverage and synthetic-only evidence do not establish universal accuracy. First-freeze truth-partitioned oracle diagnostics are labeled separately. Fractions represent post-PCR molecules/signal, not cells. All outcomes, truth, raw AB1 files, input hashes, frozen runners and plots are preserved in the Desktop PathStudy folder.

Primary sources and distinctions
TIDE: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4267669/ — shifted-control decomposition within an edit-size range.
ICE: https://github.com/synthego-open/ice — proposed repair models, including single-locus multiplex guide editing; not equivalent to joint homeolog inference.
DECODR: https://doi.org/10.1089/crispr.2020.0022 — staged model selection and residual-informed insertion inference.
BCV: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0054835 — reference dictionary and hidden-state alignment framework.
Champuru: https://eeg-ebe.github.io/Champuru/faq.html — restricted two-template reconstruction from forward and reverse reads.

2026-10-29 case-by-case failure audit and v3 indel-origin setting
The v2 improvements were applied to the original 144 audit inputs: QC passed 60 (all within the prespecified truth tolerances), versus 47 previously. These inputs informed development and are not independent validation. Of the original 97 failures/withheld cases, 15 became QC-passing and 2 formerly passing cases became withheld. A fresh 144-case test used disjoint molecule/noise seeds and a new constructed backbone: v1 QC 48 (3 outside truth tolerances), v2 QC 56 (3 outside tolerances). Full outcomes remain in the Desktop FailureAudit folder.

At the user's request, v3 adds editStartRadius (default UI setting 5 bp, optional unrestricted) to the same improved optimizer. Insertion sites must lie within the cut-proximal zone; deletion intervals may extend in either direction and must intersect the zone. Substitutions of equal length are not indels. This is a causal-origin model assumption, not observation of the physical initiation process. Existing maximum deletion/insertion limits still apply. The 5-bp run gave QC 54/144 with 3 outside tolerances on audit data; QC 48/144 with 0 observed outside tolerance on fresh data. This is a coverage/accuracy tradeoff and not evidence of universal error-free performance. Source was frozen before those runs; no further optimization used fresh truth.

Window check: a conservative reference-axis independence rule expands each W-bp target window on both sides by max(D,I)+R to cover either deletion direction and bounded phase displacement. Non-overlap with at least one separating base requires cut spacing >= W+2*max(D,I)+2R+1. This is not a biological minimum guide distance and does not disable the joint decoder when windows overlap. Unrestricted origins have no finite guaranteed cut-spacing bound. Suggested smaller window/deletion values recompute this budget. Influence regions and nominal windows are displayed separately.

Optional PCR forward/reverse primers are validated against the full product ends and carried in primer-design analysis templates. This catches reference-boundary input errors; it adds no new sequence information when the supplied amplicon is already exact. It does not establish experimental primer specificity. The sequencing primer remains a separate required input.

2026-10-29 v4 rescue study (supersedes inference/QC descriptions above)
Known-sequence and known-flank attempts share the same registration, calibration and bounded affine path search. A sequence-preserving normalization removes neutral cancelling edits and trims exact replacement context. Hybrid discovery seeds supported single-base insertion candidates near the cut. Known-sequence inference cannot certify selected unresolved N insertions. If it is not certified, a second attempt fits mixture coefficients only on known flanks while representing insertion-scaffold bases as pooled nuisance probabilities. Unknown cycles plus adjacent context are excluded from coefficient fitting and conditioning checks. At least max(100, 5*model count) known fit cycles are required and the unknown mask may not cover more than 40% of the region. Ns remain unresolved in output; pooled base probabilities are not unique inserted haplotypes.

The fallback uses all available known cycles. Even the split-position attempt can refine candidates using all observed channels, so its displayed reconstruction score is not independent cross-validation. The legacy heldOutR2 field is retained for compatibility and the UI labels signal-path results as reconstruction R2. Independent validation means new chromatogram realizations generated after source freeze.

A similarly fitting competing path, using a control-residual reconstruction allowance, can support the same reference shares and net-size spectrum while disagreeing on target editing by over 5 pp. Such target percentages are withheld. Sequence-equivalent repeat junctions are inspected beyond the paths found by the optimizer; changes in target attribution and deletion endpoints spanning both cut-origin zones also withhold target claims. These are conservative engineering identifiability checks, not calibrated significance tests. Net-size/fraction evidence is retained and must be distinguished from complete target/haplotype certainty.

Window check additionally reports whether the configured deletion bound covers the distance between two cuts plus two origin radii. This is separate from the window-independence rule. The suggested larger deletion bound is applied only by the user. Four prior wheat excision stress inputs exceeded their originally configured 200-bp bound; those original results are retained and the larger-bound retest is reported separately. Tests with 80-bp insertions exceed the configured 30-bp limit and the 60-bp prototype UI maximum; they are not relabeled as supported inputs.

All current-study inputs, full case audit, discarded variants, source freezes, exact native external-tool outputs and validation summaries are preserved in Desktop/CasMANGO_Sanger_Rescue_20261029. Original and previously fresh datasets became development data. An additional 144-pair round exposed target-attribution errors and also became development evidence. The final 144-pair round uses backbone seed 880117 and molecule/noise seed base 4900000 after the final source freeze. Wheat guide-pair conditions reuse one genomic triad; synthetic validation does not establish experimental specificity or instrument-calibrated performance.

Actual external probes in this rescue study: Tracy 0.9.1 ran 432 native decompositions (144 samples against 3 references); output creation was not scored as successful three-reference group recovery. ICE original ABI inputs exposed a PCON element-type compatibility issue; metadata-only conversion preserved signal, calls, qualities and peak payloads, after which all synthetic pairs were rejected by its quality screen. Synthetic Phred is not instrument calibrated and no QC bypass was used. These out-of-assay-scope probes do not support a general superiority claim. DECODR and PtWAVE ideas were studied but their native tools were not executed in this study.

Latest v4 consensus policy (supersedes the control-residual allowance)
The 880117/4900000 round exposed four accepted errors: three event-path/target-attribution errors despite correct net-size groups, and one genuine reference-share/net-size failure. Those outcomes are preserved as intermediate development evidence. The next policy uses any converged primary reconstruction with R2 >=0.995 as a competing explanation, without discarding it merely because a fallback has a somewhat higher R2. A fallback must agree on reference shares within 3 pp, net-size total variation within 5 pp, and target marginals within 5 pp. Conflicting estimates for the affected reference are withheld; model groups remain inspectable. These engineering policies do not prove uniqueness or establish calibrated statistical confidence.

Source was frozen in frozen-consensus/ before a new 144-pair test with backbone seed 1100357 and molecule/noise base seed 5900000. Four assay/method jobs ran concurrently, each case in a separate process with a 60-second wall budget. Runtime is not a fair speed comparison under concurrent execution. The default staged analyzer remains unchanged.

Annotation inspection: unconfirmed model groups can be explicitly expanded when fitted reference share is at least 2%; their target/sequence estimates remain labeled unresolved. Very-low-share models stay suppressed. Inferred insertion letters are displayed when specified, while N remains unresolved. This display option does not alter fit fractions, QC, or reported certainty.

Latest frozen-consensus results (same error limits, all cases retained): original development inputs v3 certified 54/144 with 3 outside truth tolerances; consensus certified 67/144 with 0 observed outside tolerances. This adds 23 formerly unaccepted correct cases and withholds 7 formerly certified-correct cases. The newest independent 144-pair test gave v3 33 certified with 4 outside tolerances (29 certified-correct), versus consensus 63 certified with 0 observed outside tolerances. Eighty-one newest-test pairs remain withheld or error; six inference errors/timeouts are retained. All-output reconstruction accuracy is not the same as usable certified coverage. Zero observed errors in this finite synthetic set does not imply universal accuracy, exact inserted haplotypes or wet-lab validation.

2026-10-29 window-role and generalisation audit (supersedes the old window cutoff)
Signal-path preparation now retains downstream signal through usable readEnd, clipped at actual coverage. The protected upstream registration/calibration prefix remains; large deletions extending into that prefix are still an unresolved architectural case. Window only defines the post-fit reporting scope and plots. It no longer changes the signal-path observation endpoint. Search limits and indel-origin priors remain separate inference settings.
A controlled test used eight physical sample/control pairs with one actual edited site, each analysed with one or two configured targets. The old observation cutoff made a first-site-only sample's maximum within-reference size-distribution TV about 35 pp with one configured target versus about 1.3 pp with an unused second target. The full usable-range correction made both under 1 pp for the legacy-noise realization. These outputs still failed the original conservative QC; raw spectrum recovery is not certified allele recovery. This is evidence of an observation-range implementation defect, not proof that target count intrinsically causes failure.
All previously inspected sets are development data. A new 96-case cross-locus/cross-waveform set exposed range truncation and became development evidence after inspection. A subsequent frozen 72-case evaluation used two new public genomic reference groups plus a new constructed backbone. Window-only correction gave 10 correct-QC share/size/target results versus 8 baseline; among 8 fully specified sequence compositions, 1 was inaccurate. The broader experimental group/cut-prior/control-response prototype gave 16 complete correct-QC share/size/target results, with 1 inaccurate specified sequence composition among 14. It was not promoted. These finite synthetic outcomes do not establish robust exact-allele inference.
The old 63/144 result was a share/net-size/target endpoint, not exact-DNA accuracy. This audit found 10 inaccurate specified observable-sequence compositions among the 54 such baseline outputs. No old case or failed variant was removed. Primary comparisons and complete inputs, failed strategies, frozen binaries, hashes and outcomes are in Desktop/CasMANGO_Sanger_Generalisation_20261029.
