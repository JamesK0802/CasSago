CasMANGO shared-primer Sanger design prototype
Research and implementation notes — 28 October 2026

Requested assay
One identical sequencing oligonucleotide and one mixed Sanger reaction. Each
homeolog must support that same oligonucleotide at a position producing a
different distance to its corresponding editing target. No homeolog-specific
sequencing primer mixture, barcoded sequencing primers, or separate sequencing
reactions are substituted for this requirement.

The opposite PCR primer is a separate role. A conventional PCR pair can use
the shared sequencing oligo and one common opposite oligo, followed by cleanup
and Sanger sequencing from the shared oligo alone. The PCR partner is never
presented as a second sequencing primer.

References consulted
1. Untergasser et al. 2012. Primer3—new capabilities and interfaces.
   https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gks596
   https://primer3.org/manual.html
   Borrowed design principles: primer length, GC, melting temperature,
   secondary-structure checks, product geometry and reuse of design settings.
   Primer3's implementation and thermodynamic secondary-structure engine are
   NOT included or claimed to run in this browser prototype.
2. Ye et al. 2012. Primer-BLAST: A tool to design target-specific primers
   for polymerase chain reaction.
   https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-13-134
   https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/tools/primer-blast/
   Candidate generation must be followed by a binding-site and pair-geometry
   check. This prototype searches only user-supplied references. It does not
   run BLAST or establish genome-wide or mismatch-tolerant specificity.
3. SantaLucia 1998. A unified view of polymer, dumbbell, and oligonucleotide
   DNA nearest-neighbor thermodynamics.
   https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.95.4.1460
   Implements a perfect-match DNA/DNA nearest-neighbour Tm calculation with
   initiation, terminal A/T and self-complementarity terms, plus a monovalent
   entropy correction. Na+ and oligo concentration are explicit. Mg2+, dNTP,
   mismatch duplex thermodynamics and primer modifications are not modelled.
4. Brinkman et al. 2014. Easy quantitative assessment of genome editing
   by sequence trace decomposition (TIDE).
   https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gku936
   Related precedent for fitting editing-induced shifts in a mixed trace.
   Its published validation is not evidence validating this homeolog assay.
5. Hill et al. 2014. Poly Peak Parser: Method and software for identification
   of unknown indels using Sanger Sequencing of PCR products.
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4525701/
   https://github.com/jonathonthill/sangerseqR
   Related reference-assisted interpretation of overlaid allele traces.
   The existing two-allele method is not treated as a general solution for
   several edited homeologs or several simultaneous editing targets.

Implemented search
- Accept full genomic/gene references up to 2,000,000 bp each and 8,000,000
  bp total (2–8 references). Enumerate A/C/G/T oligos near the targets within
  the chosen read/product geometry, in both orientations. Scan entire input
  sequences for these candidates; distant extra exact sites also reject them.
  Only the sequencing side requires different target offsets. A common
  opposite PCR primer may have identical target-relative positions.
- Filter on Na-only NN Tm, GC, homopolymer runs, contiguous self-complement
  runs, 3-prime complement runs, and simple hairpin stem length.
- Require exactly one exact site per supplied reference, checking both
  strands and overlapping occurrences. Every accepted primary primer has
  one shared reading orientation across references.
- Require every complete target window to fall downstream of the primer
  and within the user-defined usable extension range. Do not silently crop
  a window at a reference boundary.
- Compare primer-to-cut distances for each corresponding target separately.
  Different FASTA origins alone do not count as useful separation.
- Report pairwise minimum WT sequence differences in the common observed
  extension range. When a PCR pair is selected, extension sequences stop
  at the PCR product boundary, not the end of the input genomic reference.
- Report whether the corresponding cut-offset intervals overlap under the
  chosen +/-indel geometry range. This does not simulate chromatogram
  physics or establish identifiability of a mixture model.
- Search for a common, unique exact-match opposite PCR partner. All required
  windows must lie between the two primers in every reference, all product
  sizes must be within limits, and pair Tm/complement checks must pass.
- The partner stage evaluates at most 100 ranked primary candidates and
  returns at most 24 designs. A negative after hitting this bound is labelled
  an incomplete shortlist search, not impossibility.
- Work runs in a cancellable web worker. Draft inputs are stored in the
  browser's session storage. FASTA, CSV and versioned JSON exports are local.

Limits of the feasibility status
This is an exact-site candidate finder, not an experimentally validated
assay or a complete Primer3/Primer-BLAST replacement. Extra sites with
mismatches can still prime; sites outside supplied references are not checked.
Unknown bases are not accepted as exact primer matches. Hairpin/dimer screens
are heuristics, not calculated structure free energies. Different extension
efficiencies can change observed trace proportions. No AB1 parser, peak
decomposition engine, homeolog editing percentages, or NGS read analysis is
newly implemented by this feature.

Necessary next validation
Obtain separate WT traces for the intended homeolog products using the same
sequencing primer and matched chemistry. Test known mixtures before edited
samples. Evaluate extra priming, PCR representation, trace alignment/mobility,
and whether alternative homeolog-by-indel combinations produce equivalent
signals. Add controlled indel mixtures and compare to molecule-resolved NGS
before treating coefficients as homeolog-specific editing estimates.

Engineering verification
Tests cover genuine target-distance changes vs coordinate-origin changes,
both orientations, duplicate sites, complete multi-target window coverage,
PCR product geometry, no-candidate outcomes, indel-offset warnings, FASTA
validation, guide/PAM cut coordinates, worker termination, stale-result
invalidation, current-analysis imports and navigation state isolation.

Separate-homeolog PCR workflow (added 28 October 2026)
- Select a separate pair per reference, with all required windows inside the
  product and the selected sequencing side covering every target window.
- At least one primer must be specific to its homeolog under the selected
  ungapped mismatch screen (0, 1 or 2 substitutions). An option requires both
  primers to pass. The other side may use a common sequence/position.
- Screen both strands of every full input. A candidate must have exactly one
  qualifying site in its own reference and no qualifying site in other
  inputs if labelled specific. Inputs with N are rejected in this workflow
  because unknown bases cannot establish absence of near binding.
- m+1 disjoint exact seeds retrieve all possible ungapped matches with at
  most m substitutions; full oligo comparison then counts mismatches.
- Candidate generation/quality ranking is followed by a bounded check of up
  to 180 primaries and 180 partners per orientation per homeolog, returning
  up to 3 pairs each. Missing homeologs are listed; no bounded negative is
  labelled impossible. Biological specificity still needs validation.
- No gapped-binding, mismatch-thermodynamic or genome-database search.
- Supplementary design reference: Ramirez-Gonzalez et al., PolyMarker,
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4765872/
  Its polyploid locus-specific design principle informed this option;
  PolyMarker itself and its aligner/Primer3 pipeline are not executed.

Position inspection
Maps align on the selected target boundary, with shared scale, primer
orientation, opposite primer, required windows, actual genomic coordinates
and a full-reference position strip. A horizontal sequence inspector can
focus on the target, sequencing primer or opposite primer. It displays
input-strand bases; target anchoring is not a homology alignment.

3′ diagnostic SNP mode and wheat simulation (28 October 2026)
The default separate-PCR method now aligns target-anchored homologous regions,
requires a diagnostic SNP in the last 3 bases (or terminal base only) against
every other supplied homeolog, and prioritizes those primers before the bounded
shortlist. Full-input near sites with a compatible terminal segment reject a
specific primer. Shared opposite primers remain possible. Reverse-primer SNPs
are reported in oligo orientation with positions on the original inputs.
PolyMarker (Ramirez-Gonzalez et al. 2015; doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btv069)
provides the published precedent for 3′ genome-specific variation. This is our
own candidate algorithm, not execution or validation of PolyMarker/Primer3.
The alignment is target anchored, local (up to ±1,600 bp), gapped; primer binding
screens remain ungapped. A terminal mismatch does not guarantee selective PCR.

The shared-primer PCR partner also must retain every template beyond the latest
required target window on the common extension axis, including shifted models.
Checking each product only around its own cut was insufficient for pooled fitting.

The Sanger Analysis tab and ABIF mixed-trace prototype were subsequently added;
earlier 'no AB1 engine' wording above describes the original design-only stage.
An independent forward simulation and reproducible wheat harness now live in
scripts/sanger-wheat/. Source assembly: Chinese Spring IWGSC GCA_900519105.1.
Simulation results, detailed methods and source hashes are separate artifacts,
not biological validation. All 21 chromosome types are catalogued; full-genome
PCR specificity and gRNA off-target screening were not performed.

Live Ensembl reference import (28 October 2026)
Both gRNA Design and Primer Design now support on-demand genomic reference imports.
Selected species/cultivar, assembly, genomic coordinates, retrieval date and SHA-256
travel with imported references. Check latest stages source changes for explicit
