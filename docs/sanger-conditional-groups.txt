Two-target sequence groups: implemented inference contract

The fast branch supports two ordered forward targets, edit origins within +/-3 bp,
length limits up to 10 bp, separated local windows, and validated unit-cycle
emissions. It uses measured control and sample chromatograms. Unsupported inputs
retain the general analysis fallback. Synthetic truth is never an inference input.

1. Use only the measured prefix before the earliest possible second-target edit.
   Infer first-target length marginals from invariant flanks, then local sequence
   candidates from frequency atoms and bounded edit paths. Keep complete first-
   target allele backgrounds, rather than treating downstream phase as WT.

2. Fit cumulative length marginals on invariant signal after both targets. Invert
   the first-target shift convolution to propose second-target profiles. This
   initializer approximates independent pairing. Approximate local proposals are
   not accepted results: they are ranked against the original complete signal.
   Default proposal limits are five non-WT alleles per target, a 1% within-reference
   atom floor, substitution tracts up to 10 bp, and 200 proposal states per reference.

3. Materialize complete molecules for all pairings of a first-target background
   and a proposed second-target allele. With Phi[h,i,j] the complete unit-emission
   column, fit nonnegative w[h,i,j] with sum(w)=1 to all usable measured cycles.
   No constraint w[h,i,j]=p[h,i]*q[h,j] is imposed. The joint fit can represent
   correlated editing. Search candidate profiles by this same full-signal loss
   until stable (at most four passes). This is bounded candidate search, not a
   claim of exhaustive global sequence optimization. Reference shares and target
   marginals are sums of the fitted molecule weights. T1 candidate sequences are
   established from the prefix; their weights are jointly refitted.

4. Check control agreement, convergence, and per-cycle unexplained signal. Signal
   fit QC and sequence identifiability are distinct. Poor matrix conditioning is
   reported; it does not by itself prove a failed fluorescence reconstruction.

Default sequence reporting

Keep the fitted sequence groups and their point fractions. Different substitution
alleles and different deletion placements are not pooled into an N sequence.
N is permitted for a certified insertion fiber only: same canonical insertion
position, same inserted length, exactly conserved family mass, and unchanged
non-family genotype masses. Every alternative must pass complete fitted-emitter
rational equality, not merely a rounded R-squared or similar objective value.
Verified identical final deletion sequences may retain placement alternatives.
Broad substitution/WT transfers and cross-class exchanges remain research helpers;
they are not default discovery or default N/mass-range rendering.

Benchmark evaluation

The public score is a direct comparison of the current inferred sequence groups
and point fractions. A group is sequence-detected only when its sequence is
actually called above the display threshold. GT sequence detection is blue;
missing/unresolved GT sequences are red. Detection does not guarantee an accurate
fraction: estimated percentages and their deltas are evaluated separately.
An N insertion block does not earn recovery credit for its possible GT words.
The usual fraction accuracy audit uses a 2 percentage-point tolerance.

Complete-profile fiber membership is an explicitly separate research diagnostic.
It does not earn detection/recovery credit, replace a missed group, select a
representative from GT, or appear as an Option match badge. Earlier compatibility
reports must not be interpreted as complete genotype recovery rates. The corrected
point-detection audit is benchmarks/point-display-20261104/detection-audit.json.

Result navigation

Reference/target selection does not start an analysis worker. Summary rows,
reference tabs, chart inputs, annotation views, and separated raw traces are
cached for the immutable result and view parameters. Switching to an existing
target reuses those data; changed results or windows invalidate their caches.

Validation uses ideal synthetic traces. It is not a general claim about noisy
instrument data or unsupported observation models.
